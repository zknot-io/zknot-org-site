The map behind the wall

Which federal registration needs which other one first, for a small company going after SBIR or STTR money. Drawn after walking into most of it.

By William Shane Wilkinson · Salt Lake City

In The wall I wrote about touching thirteen government websites in two weeks. A commenter on LinkedIn named the actual problem: the cost is the sequence, and nobody publishes it. You find each dependency by hitting it.

So here is the sequence as I found it. It comes from one small company, one EPA proposal and one Air Force proposal still in progress. Some of this I walked, some I only read, and a few steps I haven’t reached yet. The map shows which is which, because you should trust those differently.

The same order, as a list

  1. Have the company, the EIN and a Login.gov account. SAM.gov validates your taxpayer number, and SAM.gov, SBIR.gov and DSIP all sign in through Login.gov. WALKED
  2. Register in SAM.gov, fully. You can get a UEI with just a name and address, but a full registration asks for far more, including your bank routing number. If you have no CAGE code, one gets assigned here. Everything below waits on this, so start it well ahead of any deadline. WALKED
  3. Register in the SBIR.gov firm registry. It needs your UEI and gives you an SBC Control ID. Agencies ask for that ID on proposals. WALKED
  4. Open the portal for your agency. FedConnect needs an active SAM record, your UEI and an email that matches a SAM point of contact. DSIP is the only way to file a DoD proposal. Other agencies use other portals, so read your solicitation first. WALKED
  5. If it’s civilian, you may be done. My EPA proposal needed nothing further on this map. One PDF, one portal, receipt in a minute. WALKED
  6. If it’s DoD, check whether you need a DD Form 2345. That’s the certification for receiving export-controlled military technical data. My topic asked for it or evidence of applying. If yours does, start here at least several weeks out, because the next steps depend on each other and on other people. WALKED
  7. Write your security plan and do the NIST SP 800-171 self-assessment. This happens outside any portal, and it’s real work. The DoD scoring method allows no score without a plan. READ
  8. Get into PIEE. It’s the gateway to SPRS. It needs an account administrator for your CAGE code (PIEE calls this the CAM), who approves your SPRS Cyber Vendor User role. My CAGE code had no group in the system, and the fix was an email to a help desk. PARTLY WALKED
  9. Post the score in SPRS. SPRS only stores the result, along with dates and the date your remaining gaps close. NOT REACHED
  10. Now apply through the JCP portal. Emailed and mailed forms are no longer accepted, even though the form still gives an address. JCP told me in writing to post the NIST assessment before starting the application. I didn’t find that order on any public page I could read. REFUSED, THEN TOLD

What changed this year

Older guides will tell you third-party CMMC assessments start being required on 10 November 2026. The Department of War suspended that phase in July, and a contracting memo dated 3 September told contracting officers to take third-party assessment requirements out of contracts. As reported, self-attestation against NIST standards is still required. A 60-day review was due in September, and I didn’t find a published result. Check the current position before you plan around any of this, and read your own solicitation.

Self-assessing doesn’t mean going easy on yourself. The same report describes a roughly $500,000 Justice Department settlement with a company whose near-perfect self-assessment score didn’t survive a DoD audit.

What I’d do differently

Put SAM.gov’s renewal date on a calendar the day you register. Start the DoD security work before you pick a topic, because it sits underneath the export-control form and it’s the longest job here. Use a company email address on every account from the start, since accounts opened on a personal address keep sending their mail there. And when a government form tells you where to send it, assume that may be out of date and check the portal first.
