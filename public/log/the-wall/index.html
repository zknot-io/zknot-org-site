The wall

This week I submitted one federal grant proposal and kept building another. Thirteen websites, two help desks and one rejected email later, here is what the wall around government contracting looks like from a one-person company.

By William Shane Wilkinson · Salt Lake City · Approx. 5 minutes

On Tuesday night I submitted a proposal to the EPA's small business research program. It's one PDF. Thirty pages, five signatures, uploaded through one portal. It went in at 6:33 in the evening, and the receipt came back the same minute.

That part was easy. Getting to the point where I was allowed to upload it was not, and the Air Force proposal I'm building next to it is harder still.

Thirteen

I counted. Between the EPA proposal, an Air Force research proposal due later this month, and a small Utah grant that rides on the Air Force one, ZKNOT has had to use thirteen separate websites and portals in the last two weeks. Some need an account and a second factor. Some need a certificate you can only get from another one. Two of them could only be reached by emailing a help desk and waiting.

None of them talk to each other. The same company name, address and ID numbers go into each one by hand, and each checks them against a different copy.

One afternoon of it

The Air Force topic involves export-controlled technical data. To receive that kind of data a company needs a DoD certification called a DD Form 2345. I filled it out, signed it, scanned it and emailed it to the office that issues it. The form's own instructions, on the copy I downloaded that day, say: submit your completed form as a PDF attachment to this email address.

The next morning they wrote back. They no longer accept emailed or mailed forms. Use the portal.

So I made an account on the portal. Before it lets you apply, it runs a checklist. One line says that if you haven't posted a cybersecurity self-assessment to a different DoD system, the application will be rejected.

That system is reached through a third one, a DoD-wide login. I started registering there and got an error: there is no "group" for our company's ID number, so nobody from our company can register. The fix is to email a help desk and ask them to create one.

So, for one form, four systems deep: the form, the portal, the score, the login. Plus a help desk ticket to open the login. I sent the ticket Wednesday.

The score

The cybersecurity self-assessment scores a company against 110 security requirements. It starts at 110 and loses points for every requirement not yet in place, down to minus 203.

I wrote our security plan as the company actually is today, not as I plan to build it. The honest score is low. A low score is allowed. A false one is not. The checklist also asks for a yes or no on whether we fully comply with three contract clauses. Today the true answer is no, so instead of guessing what a "no" does to the application, I emailed and asked. They answered in three minutes: post the score first, then you can say yes.

What I'm not saying

Every one of these walls exists for a reason. Export-controlled data should go to companies that have agreed to protect it. A company that handles defense data should know how secure it is. I agree with all of it, and I'm building the security work anyway because the contract would need it.

What I'm saying is that you find each wall by walking into it. Nothing lists them in order. The office that rejects your email doesn't tell you about the login two systems later. A large contractor has a department for this. A one-person company has whoever is awake.

One more thing: I'm a service-disabled veteran, and ZKNOT is certified as a service-disabled veteran-owned small business. That status opens set-aside contracts. It does nothing for these research programs, which are open to any small business. I didn't know that when I started either.

What I did about it

I made a list. Every system, what it's for, which program needs it, whether it's done, and when it expires. It turns out the same six or seven items show up under almost every program, and the rest are one-offs that only one bid asks for. Writing it down is the only reason I can see that.

The email that got rejected is on our own rail as ZK-LL6P-TTV. You can check it at verifyknot.io. It's a record of a form being sent the way the form said to send it, the day before the office said it doesn't take that anymore.
